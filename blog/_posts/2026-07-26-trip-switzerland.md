This late May my family and I traveled to Switzerland for the first time. This is my memoir on it.

> Some pictures here might just be out of my phone or my camera's JPEG preview, i.e they might look like crap. Full post-processed images will slowly make their way unto my [photography website](https://photo.electro707.com).


# Day 0, Flight Day
First day of traveling is always exciting, and this was no different. First things though, driving to the airport that is 2-3 hours away. Exciting!
After dropping off my family at the airport, I went to an off-site parking, "Purchase Park 2 Fly", to drop off the car and take the shuttle back to reconvene. As a foreshadow on the parking, it was ok, nothing exciting happened to the car (and that's a good thing). The shuttle to/from the airport was also fine.
After checking in, going through TSA, and waiting for our flight that departed at ~17:00, it was flight time to Dublin with a 3 hour layover! Fun!

When we booked the flight, a direct flight was almost double the cost of a layover flight. We chose a 3 hour layover going to Swiss and 4 hours back both in order to not miss the second flight, and because that was one of the cheaper ones available.

Landing at Dublin at 4:00am and trying to stay awake for the second flight to Swiss, we were directed to Gate E, which required a shuttle from the main gates area to this departure only area.

While trying to do something for the 3-2 hours we needed to wait, I bought my eSIM for Swiss. We went with a DigitalRepublic eSIM as it was pretty cheap. We are talking 10$CHF for unlimited data for a month at 10MBps. Other eSIM services had plans such as 30$CHF for 20Gb or something like that, but DR was unbeatable.
In order to get a Swiss eSIM, due to regulations I needed to present an ID and a face scan, which I did so over the web with some ID checking service I do not remember what was called. While normally it's supposed to activate after 5 minutes, mine did not. Seems my face was too pretty for the auto-checking system :)

With that settled though and awaiting confirmation on my eSIM, next up was our secondary flight to Swiss at 7am. This was a short 2 hour flight, the seats were OK. There was no entertainment screen on the plane, but expected for a short European flight I suppose.

Landing at Zurich Airport, a shuttle took us from the plane to the airport where we waited for customs. This was sort of long, I think 30-45min of wait time. This was with the "expedited" line if one has a biometric passport, ones with a logo on the bottom-center.
We almost were going to be told to back to the start of the line when we arrived at the customs personnel, as we are supposed to have the passport scanned and fingerprint given at a kiosk machine before joining the line. We almost missed this as it wasn't obvious with the signs around, until a personnel came around and was asking people if they did.

Finally out of immigration, it was officially off to the start to the trip. First step is to get to Wengen. Back during planning the trip, I deduced that the SBB Half-Pass was the better option than the full pass, given what we would be doing. In some locations the full pass only acted like a half pass, mainly in the Jungfrau area we would be staying at for 3 days, so that would be mute there.
The half-pass costed 150$CHF each at the SBB store at the airport, but it did end up paying for itself throughout the trip.

After navigating and understanding the train and timeline we should be taking, we took the train out of the airport to Zurich HB, where we would be transferring to a train that took us to Interlaken, and from there another train to Wengen. The train was wonderful and clean. On the longer ride from Zurich to Interlaken, we sat at the second floor of the train, something SBB trains have even for second class carts.
The train ride included some wonderful views of Swiss. Most notable was the Interlaken west lake, which was actually blue/green similar to pictures taken, but in real life! I don't think I ever say a lake this bright and vivid.

{% include blogImageFig.html img="IMG_0279.jpg" cap="blue/cyan lake" %}

After landing at Interlaken, we went to the Jungfrau tourist shop thing and purchased a 3 day Jungfrau pass. Given this is where we would be staying for the next 3 days including today, I calculated during the trip planning that this was going to be worth it. Plus it gives flexibility to take any train and cable car any time without worry for how often we do so.

From here it was two trains left, one to Lauterbrunnen, with a transfer to another train to Wengen.
The train up to Lauterbrunnen, a stop before Wengen, was fun for the first time. The view transitioned from a somewhat flat area to going in between two mountains.
The second train to Wengen from Lauterbrunnen was steep. So steep our luggage kept on going backwards; I had to apply a decent amount of force to keep them from sliding back. This is also my first time seeing a cogwheel train: a train with basically gears in the middle and on the tracks for better traction.

After arriving at Wengen station, we had to walk for 10 minutes to our first hotel, Alpenruhe Wengen. Something we did not realize when planning for the trip was how steep the city was and how much we would be walking.
Did I mention that we were more than 24 hours awake with partially sleep? Yeah we were tired. The fact we had to carry the luggage up-hill didn't help.
As a future note writing this, the hotel does offer a shuttle from the station to the hotel. Unfortunately we didn't have cellular service nor a VoIP to make that call, neither did we realize this was an option.

Arriving at the hotel, tired and sweaty, it was a wonderful small hotel. Checking in, the hotel receptionist gave us a physical key as the door key with a heavy weight attached. This would be the first hotel where an actual *classic key* was given for the door (emphasis on how the receptionist said it in a french accent).

After dropping off our stuff, the 3-bed room was lovely. Definitely big enough for 3 people and our luggage.

At this point not only were we tired, but hungry. We didn't opt for airport food, so our "meals" for the past 24 hours was some nuts and snacks and airplane food. We walked around a bit, and we settled on a pizza shop, Ristorante Da Sina. It was also past 18:00, meaning many non-bar food places were closed (something we did not realize about this area is the early store hours), and we weren't fancying a fancy restaurant. A couple of shock things we noticed while at this place: first the water isn't free. That wasn't as much of a shock as I know in Europe the water isn't free, but what was actually a shock was that if two or more people order one item for both, they will be charged a surplus. I guess restaurants expect to make some profit from each person, so two people or more ordering the same item would be eating into their margin? Another shocker is the flies flying around. My family knew about this as they talk to people in Europe. This isn't anything specific to this restaurant, just all over Europe where they don't care that much about flies flying around.
The pizza was excellent though, no complaint there.

After walking back to the hotel, we were done for the day, and finally ready to get proper rest.

Before sleeping, my DigitalRepublic eSIM was finally activated.

# Day 2, Jaungrau

Another day another lovely breakfast. I ate about the same foods as the day before.

While waiting for my sibling to be prepared, I took the hotel elevator down to the bottom floor where they had a garden area, which was nice for them to have.

{% include blogImageFig.html img="IMG_0385.jpg" cap="View from garden" %}

We planned to go up Jungfraujoch today, the "Top of Europe". It isn't actually the peak of Europe, but it is probably the most accessible one without climbing.
We took the train from Wengen to Kleine Scheidegg, where we bought the tickets up to Jungfraujoch. It's not included as part of the Jungfrau pass, but it was half-off with it. One must choose when they will be going back down with the ticket, presumably to prevent overcrowding, so we chose a 3 hour time block. We figured there's enough to enjoy up there and we did not want to be rushed.
The train up from there, after Eigergletscher, took us through inside the mountain, which was the path all the way up to Jungfraujoch. This tunnel was dug out I think sometime before the 1960s, which is quite impressive given the long length. It was also quite steep, so I wonder how much energy it takes for the train to go up, and if there are regenerative braking on the way down.

Something I noticed is there was only room for one train track. At some point it does split into two tracks, where the uphill train stops at an indoor viewing point for 5-10 minutes, which the downhill train came down during that time. This makes sense, why dig twice as much as is needed as long as the trains can be timed correctly? And this is Swiss of course, train timing is their pride.
This mid-stop viewpoint gave us the first view of some glacier outside, and the first time seeing it was quite astounding, even if it was through glass.

# Day 3, Last Minute Detour

This was our last day in Wengen, so I of course savored this last hotel breakfast. All considered with was a wonderful hotel, and if I am back in the area I definitely would be staying with them again.

At the time I knew we would miss this place. Typing this blog 2 months after the trip...I still miss it.

The original plan was to head right to Zurich and explore there for the day. While my parent and I were talking during breakfast, we came up with an unplanned for last-minute plan to stop at Zermatt instead. We took into consideration we would have all of our luggage with it, but thankfully the station provides luggage lockers when we looked it up.

So with this plan set, we headed from Wengen. We were sort of late to the train heading out of Wengen to Lauterbrunnen (with a 30 min wait for the next train). On checking out to the hotel, the receptionist asked if we wanted a drive to the station. We absolutely took on that offer, and were grateful as that lead us right to the station with more time to spare.

Down from Wengen to Lauterbrunnen down to Interlaken, we took the train to Spiez, which we then changed to a train headed for Visp. This train from Spiez to Visp initially did not look special, just another IC train, but when it entered a long tunnel section, it quickly sped up to get us to the destination faster. For some context, the train from Speiz to Visp is 26 minutes, with 10-15 minutes of that being in that high speed tunnel section. To drive, that would be 1.5 hours! Talk about some efficient trains.

From Spiez we took the longer train from Visp to Zermat. It's funny how the distance from Spiez to Visp is longer thank from Visp to Zermatt, but the train ride is half the time. This train though was slower as it went through a wavy track going alongside a mountain.

Anyways, arriving at Zermatt we put our luggage in the station locker, and went from there. Even from the ground near the station the Matterhorn was clear and visible, which was astounding to see.

Arriving at Luzern, we planned during the train ride to do the "Golden Ride", a pre-curated cable car up and a cogwheel train plus boat down from Mount Pilatus and back to Luzern. This started as a bus ride to a stop 5-10 minutes away from the Kriens cable car station. It was uphill so somewhat tiring. There the cable car took us up to the Fräkmüntegg station. During the ride we got to enjoy a lake view of Luzern from up above and the surrounding area.
